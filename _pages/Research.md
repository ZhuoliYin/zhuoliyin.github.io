Advances in AI and machine learning open new ground for data-driven forecasting, scheduling, optimization, and sequential decision-making. My research integrates AI/ML with operations research to design decision-making pipelines that are both efficient and sustainable, so that practitioners can deploy and generalize intelligent decision systems at scale. I am particularly interested in applied AI for urban mobility and logistics, finding sustainability opportunities while maintaining service efficiency.

## Current focus

### Generative AI for combinatorial optimization
Fusing generative AI with operations research to rethink how we solve hard combinatorial problems. A recent example is [ViTSP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23465) (ICLR 2026), which uses vision-language models to guide the search for large-scale traveling salesman problems. A broader theme is treating optimization as a compute-allocation problem: deciding where a finite solver budget will do the most good.

### On-demand operations of shared mobility
Learning-based models for planning and operating shared mobility systems, such as deep reinforcement learning for large-scale, online bike-share rebalancing.

### Life-cycle carbon footprint of AI systems
Measuring and reducing the environmental cost of AI, including the energy used by neural optimization solvers, so that intelligent systems deliver a net sustainability benefit.
